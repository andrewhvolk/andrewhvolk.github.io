Friends, brothers and sisters in Christ,

As we stand here in 2025, the world around us is rapidly changing. Technology has woven itself into the fabric of our daily lives, social and political landscapes are shifting, and the very ground beneath our feet seems to be trembling with uncertainty. In such times, what does it mean to be the church? What does it mean to be a Christian?

Firstly, it means embracing technology as a tool for outreach and community building. The digital world is not a threat, but a mission field. We can use social media, online platforms, and innovative apps to connect with people who may never walk through our church doors. Let us not be afraid to meet people where they are, sharing the love and grace of Christ in the digital space.

Secondly, it means standing for social justice. As Christians, we are called to defend the oppressed, to speak for the voiceless, and to work towards a world where all people are treated with dignity and respect. This means actively fighting against racism, poverty, inequality, and all forms of injustice. Let our actions reflect the heart of Jesus, who came to set the captives free.

Thirdly, it means engaging in interfaith dialogue. In an increasingly interconnected world, we must learn to listen to and understand those who hold different beliefs. This does not mean compromising our own faith, but rather seeking common ground and building bridges of understanding. Let us be known as people who love our neighbors, regardless of their background or beliefs.

Fourthly, it means practicing environmental stewardship. As stewards of God's creation, we have a responsibility to care for the earth and its resources. This means reducing our carbon footprint, conserving energy, and advocating for sustainable practices. Let us be mindful of the impact our actions have on the environment, and strive to live in harmony with nature.

Fifthly, we must be aware of the dangers of tribalism, denominationalism, and nationalism. It's natural to form communities, denominations, and nations, and to find belonging within them, but when these groups become exclusive and divisive, they can undermine the unity of the body of Christ and lead to conflict and oppression. We must strive to see beyond our own "tribes," "denominations," and "nations," recognizing that we are all part of one human family, united by our shared humanity and by our faith in Jesus. Let us celebrate our diversity while maintaining a spirit of unity and love. We must be denominations without being denominationalists, tribes without being tribalists, and nations without being nationalists.

Finally, it means remaining relevant in a rapidly changing cultural landscape. The church must be willing to adapt to new challenges and opportunities, while remaining grounded in the unchanging truth of the Gospel. This means being open to new ideas, embracing diversity, and engaging in thoughtful conversations about the issues facing our world. Let us be a beacon of hope and light in a world that is often dark and confusing.

In 2025, being the church and being a Christian means embracing technology, standing for social justice, engaging in interfaith dialogue, practicing environmental stewardship, being aware of the dangers of tribalism and denominationalism and nationalism, and remaining relevant in a rapidly changing cultural landscape. It means living out our faith in bold and transformative ways, so that the world may know the love and grace of Jesus Christ.

Amen.
