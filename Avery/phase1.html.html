Project at a glance

Project titleCode-based vs. biomarker-index definitions of NAFLD/MASLD in All of Us: phenotype burden and the diagnostic gap.
MethodologyCross-sectional analysis of adult, EHR-linked All of Us participants comparing diagnosis-code, non-invasive index (FLI/HSI; FIB-4 for fibrosis), and combined phenotypes.
PurposeShow how much observed NAFLD/MASLD burden shifts when diagnosis codes are supplemented with non-invasive indices, and who is missed.
Research questionHow do code-, index-, and combined definitions differ, and which groups are index-positive without a recorded diagnosis?
Literature gapPrior All of Us / EHR work used code-only diagnosed cases; a transparent code-plus-index comparison and diagnostic-gap estimate is missing.
TargetsPosters: The Liver Meeting (AASLD), ObesityWeek, Digestive Disease Week (DDW). Journals: Gastro Hep Advances, Hepatology Communications, BMC Gastroenterology.

Key terms

Plain-language glossary (new to this area? skim this first)

Just enough to follow Project 1. Everything below is defined here so you need no other reference.

TermPlain-language meaning
NAFLD / MASLDNonalcoholic fatty liver disease, now also called metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease - excess fat in the liver not caused by alcohol. Treat the two names as the same condition here.
SteatosisFat build-up in the liver (the "fatty" part).
NASH / MASHSteatosis plus inflammation and liver-cell injury - a more advanced stage.
FibrosisLiver scarring from ongoing injury; advanced fibrosis can progress toward cirrhosis.
FLI / HSIFatty Liver Index and Hepatic Steatosis Index - scores from routine labs and body measures that flag likely steatosis (FLI ≥ 60; HSI > 36).
FIB-4A fibrosis-risk score from age, AST, ALT, and platelets; higher means more likely advanced fibrosis.
PhenotypeHow you define, from data, that someone "has" the condition - by diagnosis codes, by an index, or both combined.
Diagnostic gapThis project's key idea: people who are index-positive but have no recorded diagnosis code.
EHRElectronic health record - clinical data (diagnoses, labs) created during medical care.
Researcher WorkbenchThe secure cloud site where you analyze All of Us data (powered by Verily); participant data never leave it.
Registered vs. Controlled TierTwo access levels. Registered Tier (codes, labs, measures, surveys, demographics) is enough for this project; Controlled Tier adds genomics, exact dates, and more granular geography/demographics, which later projects in this program need.
BucketCloud storage attached to your workspace where you save code and approved (aggregate) outputs.
Auto-pauseA setting that shuts the cloud machine down when it sits idle, so it stops billing.
CDRCurated Data Repository - the dated snapshot of All of Us data you analyze. Always record its version.
Concept setA saved group of standardized codes (concept_ids) for a clinical idea, e.g., all NAFLD diagnosis codes.
Cohort / denominatorThe participants you study; the denominator is the base group everyone is compared against.
Prevalence (observed burden)The share of the cohort meeting a definition. Call it "observed burden," not population prevalence.
Cohen's kappaA 0–1 measure of how much two definitions agree beyond chance.
Odds ratio (OR) / 95% CIFrom a logistic model: OR > 1 means higher odds; the 95% confidence interval is the plausible range.
Small-cell suppressionAll of Us privacy rule: never report a count below the program minimum (commonly 20), or a percentage that reveals one.

Paste into the workspace About tab. Keep it accurate and non-stigmatizing.

Public workspace description
This project evaluates computable definitions of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease /
metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (NAFLD/MASLD) in the All of Us
Research Program. We will compare diagnosis-code, non-invasive biomarker-index, and
combined phenotype definitions among adult participants with EHR-linked data. We will
estimate phenotype burden, code-index overlap, diagnostic gap, and subgroup variation
using aggregate, privacy-preserving outputs. Results may inform future validation and
care-cascade studies but will not identify individual participants or disclose small cells.

Resource C — novelty paragraph to adapt (for the novelty-statement step)

Start from this and make it yours; this is the framing that keeps the project from being "another code-only risk-factor table."

Novelty paragraph
Prior EHR-based NAFLD/MASLD studies, including analyses using All of Us, have shown that
diagnosed-case approaches can characterize metabolic risk factors but may miss participants
with biomarker evidence consistent with steatosis. This study addresses that ascertainment
problem by comparing diagnosis-code, non-invasive index, and combined phenotype definitions
in All of Us, then estimating the diagnostic gap among index-positive participants without a
recorded NAFLD/MASLD diagnosis. The emphasis is transparent phenotype construction,
missingness-aware interpretation, and disclosure-safe subgroup analysis.
