🤝 Math Club Interdisciplinary Team Plan

Structure, Goals, and Timeline for 2025-2026

Faculty Advisor: Mr. Andrew Volk

🎯 Mission & Core Goals

The Interdisciplinary Team acts as a bridge between the mathematical sciences and other departments at Liberty University. Our mission is to apply quantitative skills to real-world problems on campus, providing math students with practical consulting experience while offering valuable analytical support to the wider academic community.

Our primary goals for the 2025-2026 academic year are:
  • To establish consulting partnerships with at least two other academic departments.
  • To match math majors with students and faculty from other disciplines who need quantitative support for their research.
  • To complete at least one collaborative project per semester.
  • To host a "Project Pitch" event where other departments can present problems for our team to tackle.

🏗️ Team Structure & Process

Consulting Model

The team operates on a project-based consulting model. When a request is received from another department, a small team of 2-3 math students will be assigned to work directly with the client, guided by the faculty advisor.

How to Join

The Interdisciplinary Team is perfect for students who enjoy applying mathematics to diverse fields and want to develop communication and project management skills. An interest meeting will be held early in the Fall semester to outline our goals and current project opportunities.

🗓️ Proposed Semester Timeline

This schedule is focused on building relationships, sourcing impactful projects, and delivering results for our campus partners.

Fall 2025 Semester

Primary Target: Establish partnerships and launch initial projects.
  • September 2025
    Outreach & Partnership Building

    Contact department heads in fields like Biology, Business, Psychology, and Engineering to introduce the team and its services.

  • October 2025
    Interdisciplinary Project Pitch Event

    Host an event where faculty and students from other departments can "pitch" their research problems to the Math Club. Form project teams based on interest and skills.

  • November 2025
    Project Work & Initial Analysis

    Begin work on selected projects, focusing on understanding the problem, cleaning data, and performing initial analyses.

Spring 2026 Semester

Primary Target: Deliver project results and showcase collaborations.
  • January & February 2026
    Deep Dive & Model Development

    Continue project work, focusing on developing mathematical models, running simulations, or conducting in-depth statistical tests.

  • March 2026
    Final Reporting & Presentation Prep

    Finalize project results and create reports or presentations to deliver to the client departments.

  • April 2026
    Collaborative Showcase

    Host an end-of-year showcase where the Interdisciplinary Team and their partners present the results of their collaborative work to the university community.

💡 Potential Project Areas

We are equipped to help with a wide range of quantitative challenges, including:

  • Data Analysis & Visualization: Helping a research group from the School of Business analyze customer survey data.
  • Statistical Consulting: Assisting psychology students with the experimental design and statistical analysis for their senior thesis.
  • Mathematical Modeling: Building a population dynamics model for a biology class experiment.
  • Process Optimization: Working with the School of Engineering on a logistics or scheduling problem.
