Project Overview

This type of research is collaborative at its core. It breaks down the wall between the "researcher" and the "subject" by working directly with community members as partners. The goal is to produce knowledge and solutions that are directly relevant and useful to the partner organization or community, making a tangible local impact.

Common Methodologies:

  • Community-Engaged Math Modeling: Partnering with a local non-profit, school, or city department to use mathematical and statistical modeling to help them solve a practical problem (e.g., optimizing bus routes, scheduling volunteers, analyzing program data).
  • Participatory Action Research (PAR): A collaborative approach where researchers and community members work together to identify a problem, collect and analyze data, and take action to bring about change. The community members are co-researchers.

🚀 Quick Start Guide (First 2-3 Weeks)

  1. Identify a Potential Partner: Brainstorm local organizations that might have problems solvable with math (e.g., a food bank, an after-school program, a city planning office).
  2. Initiate Contact: Reach out to the organization, introduce your team, and propose a collaborative meeting to discuss their needs and challenges. The Interdisciplinary Team can be a great resource here.
  3. Co-develop a Research Question: In partnership with the community organization, define a research question or problem that is both meaningful to them and feasible for you to address.
  4. Establish a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU): Draft a simple agreement that outlines the goals, roles, responsibilities, and data ownership for both the student team and the community partner. You will also need IRB approval.

🗓️ Suggested Timeline (14-16 Weeks)

  • Weeks 1-4
    Partnership Building & Problem Definition

    Hold several meetings with your community partner to build trust and deeply understand their context and needs. Finalize the research question and MOU. Submit your IRB application.

  • Weeks 5-9
    Collaborative Data Collection & Modeling

    Work with your partners to gather the necessary data. If modeling, build the initial version of your mathematical or statistical model, getting regular feedback from the partner to ensure it reflects reality.

  • Weeks 10-12
    Analysis & Co-interpretation

    Analyze your data or model results. Crucially, hold a meeting with your community partner to interpret the findings together. Their contextual knowledge is essential for making sense of the results.

  • Weeks 13-16
    Developing Deliverables & Final Report

    Create the final deliverables for your partner (e.g., a report with recommendations, a spreadsheet tool, a presentation). Write your academic paper summarizing the entire process and its outcomes.

📄 Key Deliverables

Final Paper & Community Report

This type of research often produces two distinct deliverables:

  • Academic Paper: A traditional research paper detailing the methodology, findings, and theoretical implications of your work.
  • Community Deliverable: A practical, accessible product for your partner. This could be a one-page summary of recommendations, a user-friendly software tool, a presentation to their board, or a workshop for their staff. This is often the most important outcome.
Recommended Tools
  • Project Management: Tools like Trello or Asana can help manage tasks and communication between the student team and the community partner.
  • Data Analysis: This will vary based on the project and could include anything from Excel and statistical software (R, JASP) to optimization software (for operations research) or GIS software (for mapping).
  • Communication: Strong interpersonal and communication skills are the most important "tools" for this work.
