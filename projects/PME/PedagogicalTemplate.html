🧑‍🏫 Pedagogical & Curricular Project Template

A pedagogical project investigates curriculum design, instructional strategies, and assessment tools to improve math teaching and learning.
← Back to All Project Types

Project Overview

This type of research focuses on the practice of teaching mathematics. Instead of proving new theorems, you are contributing to the knowledge of how to teach math more effectively. It's a practical field that can have a direct impact on the classroom experience for students and instructors.

Common Methodologies:

  • Curriculum Analysis: Evaluating math curriculum, textbooks, or online materials for specific characteristics, such as the representation of real-world modeling, alignment with learning objectives, or inclusion of diverse perspectives.
  • Action Research: A cyclical process where an instructor or researcher identifies a classroom problem, implements a change in instructional practice, and assesses the results to refine the practice. It's research in action.
  • Assessment Design: Creating and validating a new tool for measuring student understanding, such as a concept inventory, a skills test, or a rubric for grading mathematical proofs.

🚀 Quick Start Guide (First 2-3 Weeks)

  1. Identify a Problem of Practice: What is a common challenge in math education you'd like to address? (e.g., "Students struggle with the transition to proof-based courses," "Textbooks don't connect calculus to real-world applications.").
  2. Choose Your Focus: Decide whether you want to analyze existing materials (curriculum analysis), test a new teaching strategy (action research), or build a new assessment tool.
  3. Draft a Proposal: Write a one-page proposal outlining your research question, your chosen method, and what you hope to find or create.
  4. Get Permissions: If your project involves observing a class or using student data, you will need permission from the course instructor and will likely need to go through the IRB approval process.

🗓️ Suggested Timeline (12-14 Weeks)

  • Weeks 1-3
    Framework & Protocol Development

    Develop your analytical framework. If analyzing a curriculum, create your coding scheme. If testing an intervention, detail the procedure. If building an assessment, draft the initial items.

  • Weeks 4-7
    Data Collection / Implementation

    Gather your materials (e.g., textbooks to analyze). If doing action research, implement your teaching strategy in a classroom setting and collect data (e.g., test scores, observations).

  • Weeks 8-10
    Analysis

    Analyze your findings. This could involve coding textbook sections, statistically comparing pre/post test scores, or analyzing the psychometric properties of your new assessment.

  • Weeks 11-14
    Writing the Report & Recommendations

    Write up your findings. The key part of a pedagogical paper is the "Implications for Practice" section, where you provide concrete recommendations for teachers based on your research.

📄 Key Deliverables

Final Paper Structure
  • Introduction: Describe the problem of practice and why it's important. State your research question.
  • Theoretical Framework/Literature Review: Ground your study in existing educational theories or research.
  • Methods: Detail your procedure for analysis, intervention, or design. Explain how you collected and analyzed your data.
  • Results: Present your findings in a clear, organized manner.
  • Discussion & Implications: Interpret your results. What do they mean for teaching and learning? Provide specific, actionable recommendations for instructors or curriculum designers.
Recommended Tools
  • Curriculum Analysis: Qualitative data analysis software (NVivo, Dedoose) or spreadsheets for coding.
  • Action Research: Statistical software (R, JASP, SPSS) for analyzing student performance data; survey tools (Qualtrics, Google Forms) for gathering student feedback.
  • Assessment Design: Psychometric analysis software (R with 'ltm' or 'mirt' packages) to perform item analysis and validation.
  • Writing: Overleaf (LaTeX) or a standard word processor.
