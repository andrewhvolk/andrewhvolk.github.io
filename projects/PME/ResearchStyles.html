🔬 LU Math Club Research Project Types

This guide provides an overview of different research methodologies you can undertake. Click on any template to learn more about its process, timeline, and required deliverables.

I. Literature-Based Projects

Synthesizing and mapping published research to identify patterns, gaps, and consensus in mathematics literature.
  • PRISMA Systematic Review: Structured synthesis of peer-reviewed literature on a focused math topic.
  • Scoping Review: Broad mapping of existing literature to identify trends and gaps.
  • Meta-Analysis: Statistical synthesis of effect sizes from multiple quantitative studies.
📚 View Literature Review Template

II. Qualitative Inquiry Projects

Exploring experiences, perceptions, and themes using interviews, observations, and text analysis.
  • Phenomenological Analysis: Study of lived experiences in learning or teaching math.
  • Grounded Theory: Data-driven theory development from interviews or observations.
  • Thematic Analysis: Identify patterns and themes in open-ended qualitative data.
  • Case Study: In-depth investigation of a specific classroom, program, or individual.
🎤 View Qualitative Research Template

III. Quantitative Research Projects

Using surveys, experiments, and statistical models to quantify relationships and predict outcomes.
  • Descriptive Statistics & Survey Research: Explore trends and group differences in attitudes or knowledge.
  • Quasi-Experimental Design: Compare outcomes between non-randomized groups.
  • Correlational Study: Explore associations between two or more quantitative variables.
  • Regression Modeling: Predict outcomes using statistical models on large datasets.
📊 View Quantitative Research Template

IV. Simulation and Computational Projects

Using computation, simulation, and algorithmic approaches to explore mathematical phenomena or solve complex problems.
  • Simulation Study: Model mathematical or statistical behavior using computational simulations.
  • Monte Carlo Simulation: Estimate integrals, probabilities, or expectations via random sampling.
  • Numerical Experimentation: Test convergence, stability, or accuracy of numerical methods.
  • Algorithm Analysis: Study complexity and efficiency of mathematical algorithms.
💻 View Simulation & Computational Template

V. Theoretical and Proof-Based Projects

Formulating and proving mathematical results, generalizing theorems, or exploring new conjectures.
  • Proof-Based Exploratory Research: Extend or generalize known mathematical results.
  • Mathematical Modeling: Model and analyze real-world systems using mathematics.
  • Number Theory Research: Explore number-theoretic questions using proofs and computation.
  • Combinatorics and Graph Theory: Prove properties of graphs, networks, or combinatorial objects.
🔢 View Theoretical & Proof-Based Template

VI. Mixed Methods Projects

Combining qualitative and quantitative approaches to provide a more comprehensive understanding of research questions.
  • Exploratory Sequential: Use qualitative findings to inform a quantitative phase.
  • Convergent Design: Collect and analyze both qualitative and quantitative data concurrently.
🤝 View Mixed Methods Template

VII. Pedagogical and Curricular Projects

Investigating curriculum design, instructional strategies, and assessment tools to improve math teaching and learning.
  • Curriculum Analysis: Evaluate math curriculum for DEI, modeling, or assessment alignment.
  • Action Research: Implement and assess changes in instructional practice.
  • Assessment Design: Create and validate a concept inventory or rubric.
🧑‍🏫 View Pedagogical Project Template

VIII. Textual and Lexical Analysis Projects

Examining language use, discourse patterns, and terminology in mathematical contexts through quantitative and qualitative techniques.
  • Quantitative Lexical Analysis: Analyze word frequency, co-occurrence, or sentiment in mathematical texts.
  • Corpus Linguistics: Study language patterns in research papers or student writing.
  • Discourse Analysis: Explore how students and instructors use language to build mathematical meaning.
📖 View Textual Analysis Template

IX. Network and Structural Analysis Projects

Investigating patterns, relationships, and structures within mathematical, social, or conceptual networks.
  • Social Network Analysis (SNA): Examine collaboration or citation networks in math communities.
  • Graph-Theoretic Explorations: Analyze real-world networks with mathematical tools.
  • Structural Topic Modeling: Extract latent themes from large text datasets using probabilistic models.
🌐 View Network Analysis Template

X. Visual and Multimodal Analysis Projects

Utilizing visual data, recordings, and multimodal artifacts to study mathematical thinking and communication.
  • Eye-Tracking or Gaze Path Analysis: Track how students read and interpret math visuals.
  • Video Ethnography of Problem Solving: Analyze recordings of student groups solving math problems.
👁️ View Visual Analysis Template

XI. Philosophical, Historical, and Conceptual Projects

Exploring foundational, historical, and philosophical questions that inform mathematics and its pedagogy.
  • Conceptual Analysis: Explore the development and interpretation of key mathematical ideas.
  • Mathematics History-Based Inquiry: Investigate historical progressions and their modern implications.
  • Philosophical Inquiry: Analyze underlying philosophical assumptions in math education or theory.
🏛️ View Philosophical/Historical Template

XII. Design-Based Research Projects

Iteratively developing, testing, and refining tools, curricula, or games to enhance mathematical learning and engagement.
  • Iterative Tool or Curriculum Design: Design and improve learning materials through research cycles.
  • Digital Math Game Analysis: Study or create games to teach math concepts through play.
🛠️ View Design-Based Research Template

XIII. Participatory and Community-Based Projects

Partnering with stakeholders to solve real-world problems or improve educational experiences through mathematics.
  • Community-Engaged Math Modeling: Partner with local groups to solve practical problems with math.
  • Participatory Action Research (PAR): Collaboratively research educational experiences to drive change.
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 View Community-Based Template

🤝 Working with Your Faculty Advisor

  • Come to meetings with a prepared agenda and specific questions. This shows initiative and respects their time.
  • Send a brief summary email after each meeting, outlining key takeaways, action items, and deadlines.
  • Provide drafts or code with enough time for them to review before your meeting (at least 24-48 hours is a good rule of thumb).
  • Be honest about your progress. If you're stuck, say so. Advisors can't help with problems they don't know about.
  • Remember their role is to guide you, not to give you the answers. Active engagement with their feedback is key.

Additional Support Resources

  • Writing Centers: Most universities offer help with academic writing
  • Statistical Consulting: Many schools have free stats consulting for students
  • Library Research Services: Librarians can help with literature searches and database access
  • Undergraduate Research Offices: Funding opportunities, conferences, and mentorship programs
  • Online Communities: Math Stack Exchange, and other online forums for conceptual questions.
  • Professional Organizations: MAA (Mathematical Association of America), SIAM (student memberships available)
  • Research Conferences: JMM (Joint Mathematics Meetings), MAA MathFest, regional undergraduate conferences
