# Learning audit and research recommendations

Date: October 31, 2026. Audited source revision: `8d84c902ad1748c03e59ce75d72f07f4959dd9f7`. Public app: https://andrewhvolk.github.io/palabra/.

Historical baseline: the subsequent [guided-learning update](GUIDED-LEARNING.md) implements simpler entry, focused daily recommendations, a three-word new-profile ceiling and a larger due-review allocation. The observations and JSON below preserve the pre-update policy. Remaining research recommendations are explicitly listed in the update; they are not all claimed as implemented.

## Assessment

Palabra has a useful foundation for a classroom supplement: contextual grammar, accent-aware feedback, explicit help flags, a provisional diagnostic, saved sessions, and honest separation of automatic assessment from personal-response self-assessment. Its highest-value next step is to improve the scheduling and interpretation of learning evidence. Adding more flashcards or raising the new-word limit would not resolve the main problems.

The strongest evidence supports attempted retrieval, practice distributed across days, corrective feedback, and later tests of retention. Evidence does **not** establish a universal ideal of 10 new words, 20 cards per set, 15 minutes, 80% accuracy, or one exact expanding interval sequence. Proposed numerical defaults below are testable product decisions.

This is a source/learning-policy audit and a focused research review, not a systematic review or a study of Palabra learners. No teaching defaults or deployed runtime were changed. The independent Spanish educator review remains pending.

Writing and speaking each have one prompt per chapter. Activity count is not a count of independent language situations.
- Existing 34 Node tests pass. Deterministic source/Pages parity passes for 25 runtime assets. Fresh anonymous HTTP reads confirmed the deployed engine, main UI, course assembly and communication module match the corresponding Pages build assets after line-ending normalization. Build-versioned imports explain source/build text differences. No new interactive browser or real classroom trial was performed for this research audit; previous release browser evidence is in VERIFICATION.md.
- [Read-only probes](research/learning-probes.mjs) reproduce the policy observations; [results](research/learning-probes.json) include synthetic cases, never student records. Run `node projects/Palabra/app/docs/research/learning-probes.mjs` from the repository root.

## Preserve these strengths

Immediate feedback explains person/tense contrasts, agreement and accents; `ñ` remains distinct from `n`. Verb practice assesses contextual forms rather than treating a reference table as an exercise. Hints, answer reveals, retries, diagnostic results and text-only listening fallbacks are excluded from independent skill evidence. Repeated same-day card assessments cannot repeatedly promote the vocabulary box. New-word reservations, captured settings, resume, undo and backups protect continuity. These are valuable safeguards to retain during scheduler migration.

Self-graded vocabulary can be useful for spoken or accessible practice, and introductory receptive practice is legitimate. Their scores need to describe what was actually demonstrated.

## Findings, in recommended implementation order

| Priority | Current behavior and consequence | Recommended change | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A 15-minute daily plan allocates only five vocabulary items. Mature cards always return after 14 calendar days. All 315 introduced through-2B cards require 22.5 reviews/calendar day at that interval; all 940 require 67.1. | Estimate due workload against available time; throttle introductions; allow longer mature intervals after successful delayed retrieval; support an explicit active/maintenance scope. Show remaining reviews without implying the plan cleared the queue. | Spacing is well supported [R1, R2]. Personalized review has direct school-Spanish evidence [R6]. The workload arithmetic is an app constraint, not an experimental result. |
| 2 | A first correct vocabulary judgment advances straight to box 2, nominally two days away. A miss resets to tomorrow. Neither automatically returns later in the same plan. Grammar offers an immediate retry while the answer remains visible, then advances. | Add bounded learning/relearning: attempted recall, explanation, a later retrieval after intervening work, and a later-day check. Preserve the original miss; distinguish supported correction from independent delayed evidence. | Retrieval and successive relearning support the mechanism [R3, R4]. Additional within-session trials have a time cost [R5]. A precise three- or five-item lag is a pilot choice. |
| 3 | Grammar scheduling prioritizes gaps/current work and least-used variants. It has no memory-based due date per objective; “secure” becomes stale after a fixed 14 days. Novelty and more same-day attempts can displace strategically timed review. | Give objectives delayed review dates and reserve a small review component alongside current instruction. Schedule by last independent retrieval, success and elapsed time, not just least-used item IDs. | Distributed practice [R1] and personalized review [R6] support the direction. Their results do not validate any particular new grammar scheduler. |
| 4 | “Secure” requires six independent answers, at least 80% accuracy, two local dates and three exercise IDs. Six correct answers on three contextual -ar items over 120 seconds across midnight meet it. Three reading questions about one passage, repeated on a second date, also meet it. | Require genuinely delayed evidence and more than one independent stimulus; retain person/tense/context breadth. Report “consistent recent practice” separately from “retained on a later check.” Reserve fluent/generalized claims for appropriate assessments. | Relearning and transfer findings support delayed, appropriately varied assessment [R4, R9]. No reviewed study validates the current six/80% rule or proves that a replacement cutoff certifies mastery. |
| 5 | Vocabulary defaults to Spanish-to-English, optional typed answer, reveal, then self-judgment. Both directions share one box/due date. Recognizing a translation can postpone practice producing Spanish. | Prompt a deliberate retrieval attempt before reveal; allow spoken/mental attempts for accessibility. Track receptive and productive evidence separately under the same stable card ID. Introduce production gradually after form/meaning support. | Repeated retrieval [R3] supports attempted recall. Direction effects vary with proficiency [R8]; forcing all beginners into production or asserting a universally optimal 50/50 split would overstate the evidence. |
| 6 | Alternating a word card with a grammar item changes activity type but does not necessarily train discrimination among related structures. Many prompts name the verb/person/tense. | After a brief explanation and examples, mix meaningful contrasts: subject/person endings, singular/plural agreement, or ser/estar in unambiguous contexts. Include tasks requiring the learner to choose the construction from meaning. Keep some controlled form practice. | L2 grammar experiments support interleaving [R7]; outcomes and learner/task conditions vary. Randomly mixing every chapter is not the intervention studied. |
| 7 | Each chapter has one reading passage and one listening stimulus. A sentence-identification item can offer unrelated sentences from other chapters; recognizing a topic can be enough. Writing/speaking models and prompts also repeat. | Expand independent stimuli before adding more near-duplicate questions. Add plausible listening alternatives differing in the target information, unseen short passages and new sentence situations. Separate trained-item accuracy from transfer-check results. | Transfer is possible but conditional [R9]. Content expansion is a design inference, not a prescribed research count of passages. |
| 8 | Minutes are converted into item counts without measured reading/writing/lesson time. At 15 minutes the initial plan has 12 activities; with prerequisites explored it can have 17. At 20 minutes it can have 23. | Calibrate estimates by activity type and actual active time; offer a natural stopping point and resumable next chunk. Include lesson reading, replaying audio, corrections and personal responses in the budget. | Set size alone is a weak basis for optimization [R10]. Exact duration and item-count defaults need classroom observation. |
| 9 | Free writing/speaking has honest self-checklists but no independent response feedback. Device TTS is the only audio source. | Preserve self-assessment labeling; add optional teacher review opportunities and reviewed audio when feasible. Use low-stakes unseen comprehension and constrained production checks alongside open responses. | Corrective-feedback evidence is relevant [R11], but human oral-feedback findings are not direct proof of a chatbot, TTS or automatic score. Teacher review remains a separate content-quality need. |

Class position also permits words from the full cumulative pool; unseen vocabulary is shuffled rather than sequenced to the active lesson. | Keep diagnostic optional and provisional; provide short saved sections and sampled-domain explanations. Select early words that support the active lesson/task, with cumulative review. A later-chapter placement should not silently imply later grammar was assessed. | Alignment is a pedagogical/product judgment here. No reviewed research proves this diagnostic, its length or its placement thresholds valid. |

### Workload reproduction and limits

The mature-deck calculation assumes every selected card has already been introduced, all answers are correct, and all are maintained in box 5. It does not mean a new student immediately owes 315 reviews. On a five-schoolday schedule, the same nominal two-week demand is about 32 reviews per schoolday through 2B and 94 through 9B. Errors and early boxes add demand.

At five reviews/day, simply touching every active card once takes at least 63 calendar days for 315 cards and 188 for 940. The due queue therefore cannot follow its nominal 14-day schedule. A 56-day synthetic maintenance run, starting with mature due dates evenly spread over 14 days, leaves 245/315 or 870/940 cards due. This is a capacity demonstration, not a prediction of actual forgetting or a claim that a finite deck's backlog grows without limit.

The app already prioritizes due items over new ones. Its `newLimit: 10` is a ceiling: the default daily path can introduce at most five and none when five due cards fill the word block. The separate words-only practice path defaults to 20. These three numbers describe different controls and should be explained accordingly.

## Proposed pilot defaults

These are starting hypotheses for a classroom pilot, **not scientifically established optimal values**. They should ship only with scheduling tests and measured retention/workload checks.

| Control | Suggested starting policy | Why / what to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Daily duration | Keep 15 minutes as the default; retain shorter/longer choices and add a five-minute option. | A useful product budget. Compare predicted time with active time and completion; there is no universal 15-minute learning optimum. |
| Word block | Start with a chunk of about 10 retrieval opportunities, extend toward 15 only if time allows. This includes due, new and relearning trials. | More review capacity than five, but not permission to introduce 10–15 new words. Preserve time for grammar and communication. This alone still cannot maintain the full deck at the current interval cap. |
| New words | Start at three/day, adjustable within a small novice range such as two–five; reduce to zero when forecast review demand consumes the word budget. | A conservative workload decision. Count new items separately from retrieval trials; let course pace/learner performance adjust it. |
| First learning and lapses | Give feedback after an attempt; return difficult items after roughly three–five intervening activities, at most one or two extra opportunities within the time budget. Check again the next day. | The retrieval/relearning mechanism is supported; the lag and retry cap are provisional. Do not require seven immediate successes on every card or loop indefinitely. |
| Subsequent spacing | Pilot approximately 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 days after successful independent checks, shortening after difficulty and extending only after delayed success. | An intelligible initial policy, not the research's optimal sequence. Apply conservatively to newly learned material; keep overdue priority and an assessment-date override from replacing normal learning. |
| Grammar | One main new pattern plus a related contrast/review, with delayed checks of older objectives. | Keep lesson coherence while practicing discrimination. Exact counts should vary with readiness and task duration. |
| Directions | Supported meaning recognition first where needed; progressively add English-to-Spanish and contextual production, with separate direction evidence. | Fits the goal of usable Spanish without assuming all novices benefit from the same ratio. |
| Progress | Preserve first-attempt accuracy; require a later-day retention check and unseen variants/stimuli before stronger retention labels. | A ≥24-hour gate is a reasonable engineering safeguard against midnight artifacts, not a universal mastery threshold. Pilot an additional check after a week. |
| Communication | Include a comprehension or application activity in each daily plan; rotate modalities across days when the time budget is short. | Avoid rushing a passage, audio and personal response merely to complete a fixed count. Periodically test a new stimulus. |

A staged adaptive scheduler is preferable to immediately replacing the engine with a fashionable algorithm. First make the current workload and learning states coherent. Then compare a simple transparent policy with a candidate adaptive model using independent delayed recall. Prediction accuracy, session accuracy and number of completed cards are different outcomes from language learning.

## Research basis and applicability

The following are primary research articles, original meta-analyses, or an authors' research review. Publisher abstracts were sufficient for some conclusions; full text was also inspected where available. This is a focused selection of relevant evidence, not an exhaustive search or pooled estimate for Palabra. Findings across populations/tasks do not guarantee the same benefit in novice classroom Spanish.

**R1 — Kim & Webb (2022), L2 spacing meta-analysis.** Forty-eight experiments (3,411 participants) support spaced practice and longer gaps for delayed outcomes. Equal and expanding spacing were statistically equivalent. This supports distributed practice, not a claim that a particular expanding sequence is superior. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479).

**R2 — Cepeda et al. (2008), retention interval and spacing.** With more than 1,350 participants learning facts, the best review gap increased with the delay to the final test. An interval should depend on the retention goal. This was not a trial of Spanish flashcards or the proposed defaults. [Author's institutional record and abstract](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/).

**R3 — Karpicke & Roediger (2008), foreign-language vocabulary retrieval.** Repeated retrieval after successful recall improved delayed performance in their paradigm; learners' predictions did not track retention well. This favors recall opportunities over relying solely on a feeling of familiarity after reveal. It does not imply initial explanation/restudy has no value. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408).

**R4 — Rawson & Dunlosky (2022), successive relearning.** Their review describes reaching successful retrieval and relearning again in spaced sessions. The evidence favors returning to learned material over time. A same-screen correction is useful support but cannot itself demonstrate durable retention. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/09637214221100484).

**R5 — Nakata (2017; online 2016), within-session repetition.** Ninety-eight Japanese learners studied 16 English–Japanese pairs. Five/seven retrievals produced higher scores than one/three, but one retrieval gave the greatest gain when time-on-task was controlled. More repetitions can improve an item while reducing efficiency; neither “once is always enough” nor “repeat everything seven times” follows. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263116000280).

**R6 — Lindsey et al. (2014), personalized review in school Spanish.** A semester-long middle-school course compared time-matched strategies; the authors report improved end-of-course retention from personalized review over massed and uniform spaced review. This is especially relevant to Palabra's workload problem, but its method and reported gains cannot be assumed for this app. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613504302).

**R7 — Nakata & Suzuki (2019), L2 grammar interleaving.** Among 115 Japanese learners studying five English structures, interleaving produced more training errors but better one-week grammaticality judgments than blocking. It supports meaningful mixed contrasts; it is not direct evidence of spontaneous spoken Spanish improvement. [Author manuscript](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Nakata-Suzuki-2019-MLJ.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12581).

**R8 — Terai, Yamashita & Pasich (2021), retrieval direction.** In a small study of 28 Japanese English learners, direction effects differed by proficiency. This cautions against a universal productive-only or fixed-ratio default. Recognition and production should be assessed and reported according to the intended language task. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263121000346).

**R9 — Pan & Rickard (2018), transfer meta-analysis.** Across 122 experiments, retrieval could support transfer, but benefits depended on task relationships and other moderators. Accurate repetition of trained items does not by itself establish broad transfer. Include appropriate novel contexts and measure them separately. [Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29733621/).

**R10 — Nakata & Webb (2016; online 2015), practice-set size.** Experiments compared four-, ten- and twenty-item groupings while controlling spacing. Group size had little effect when spacing was equivalent; spacing mattered more. Small interface chunks are reasonable, but they must not collapse every difficult word into immediate massed repetition. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263115000236).

**R11 — Lyster & Saito (2010), oral corrective feedback.** Fifteen classroom studies (827 learners) found durable benefits of corrective feedback. This supports maintaining correction and response opportunities, with a clear limitation: teacher–student oral interaction is not equivalent to automated exact-answer grading or self-scored speaking. [Author PDF](https://kazuyasaito.net/SSLA2010.pdf).

**R12 — Ruitenburg et al. (2026), authentic-school classroom meta-analysis.** Published October 29, 2026: 53 studies, 7,303 participants. Average effects favored retrieval, spacing and interleaving, with important moderators and context dependence. It corroborates the broad principles while cautioning against transplanting a one-size-fits-all policy. Its recency is not a reason to replace the more directly relevant L2 evidence. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10225-5).

## Verification and implementation gates

Suggested sequence: (1) review/time budget and visible workload; (2) bounded learning/relearning plus versioned scheduler migration; (3) per-objective delayed checks and stronger evidence labels; (4) direction evidence and meaningful contrasts; (5) expanded stimuli/teacher review; (6) outcome evaluation. Validate and release each change before starting the next, consistent with the project's sequential workflow.

Preserve all vocabulary IDs and existing export compatibility. Do not silently reinterpret historical self-judgments as typed production, fabricate unseen-stimulus evidence, overwrite pending sessions, or bulk-move existing due dates on import. Add explicit scheduler policy versions and migration checks when implementation begins. Accounts are not needed to do any of this: keep local-only use; optional future Palabra/SQL accounts can store the same evidence.

Tests should cover first learning, lapses, bounded queue growth, once-per-day box advancement, independent versus supported rechecks, delayed objective review, school/weekend backlog, mature intervals, time-budget stopping, separate directions, midnight/DST, old backups and pending-session resume. Existing passing tests establish conformance to the current policy, not that the policy optimizes retention.

A small classroom pilot should measure: independent recall after seven and 28 days, trained versus unseen sentence/stimulus performance, review time per retained item, actual session duration, completion and overdue age. Include productive and receptive outcomes; record replay/hint use. Prefer time-matched comparisons and counterbalanced item sets where feasible. Report sample sizes and uncertainty; immediate accuracy or streaks alone cannot establish an improvement. Optional, minimized local timing/evidence export is enough to begin; no cloud logs, paid API or Google account is required.

Remaining evidence gaps: no Palabra learner outcome trial, no calibrated response-time data, no validated diagnostic/mastery cutoffs, limited independent stimulus breadth, pending independent Spanish educator review, and unverified classroom audio quality. The numeric defaults above remain proposals until implemented and evaluated.
