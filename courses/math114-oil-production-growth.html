Oil Production Growth, Step by Step

Use the exponential growth model to estimate world oil production at different growth rates, compare the estimates with the actual 2020 value, and then work backward to the growth rate that really happened. Try each part first, check your answer, and open the steps when you need them.

The problem

What are we given?

In 1950, production was about500 million tonswhich is 0.5 billion tons.
From 1950 to 1971 it grew about7.6% per yearthat is, r = 0.076.
In 2020, actual production was about4.6 billion tonsthe value to compare against.

Use the exponential growth model, with every amount in billions of tons:

A = P(1 + r)t

P is the starting amount, r the yearly growth rate as a decimal, t the number of years, and A the ending amount.

Write rates as decimals: 7.6% is 0.076 and 3.1% is 0.031.

Part A

Estimate production in 1971

Grow the 1950 amount at 7.6% a year until 1971. Round to one decimal place.

billion tons
Show the steps
  1. Start with the 1950 production: P = 0.5 billion tons.
  2. Use the 7.6% growth rate: r = 0.076.
  3. Count the years: 1971 − 1950 = 21, so t = 21.
A = 0.5(1.076)21 ≈ 2.328

About 2.3 billion tons in 1971.

Part B

What if 7.6% growth had continued until 2020?

Start from your rounded Part A answer and keep growing at 7.6% a year until 2020. Round to one decimal place, then compare with the actual 4.6 billion tons.

billion tons
Show the steps
  1. Start from the rounded Part A result: P = 2.3 billion tons.
  2. Use the same growth rate: r = 0.076.
  3. Count the years: 2020 − 1971 = 49, so t = 49.
A = 2.3(1.076)49 ≈ 83.3
4.6 billionactual 2020 productionis far less than83.3 billionprojected at 7.6%

About 83.3 billion tons. The actual figure is far less, so growth must have slowed after 1971.

Part C

What if the rate after 1971 was 3.1%?

Start again from the rounded Part A answer, now growing at 3.1% a year until 2020. Round to one decimal place.

billion tons
Show the steps
  1. Start from the rounded Part A result: P = 2.3 billion tons.
  2. Use the new growth rate: r = 0.031.
  3. Use the same time span: t = 49 years.
A = 2.3(1.031)49 ≈ 10.3
4.6 billionactual 2020 productionis far less than10.3 billionprojected at 3.1%

About 10.3 billion tons. Even 3.1% a year is too fast to match what happened.

Part D

What growth rate from 1971 to 2020 matches the actual 2020 value?

Find the yearly rate that takes the 1971 amount to 4.6 billion tons in 49 years. Give the rate as a percent to three decimal places.

Use the unrounded Part A value, 0.5(1.076)21 ≈ 2.328239764. Starting from 2.3 gives a slightly different rate (about 1.425%).
% per year
Show the algebra
4.6 = 2.328239764(1 + r)49
  1. Isolate the growth factor. Divide both sides by the starting amount: (1 + r)49 = 4.6 ÷ 2.328239764 ≈ 1.975741533. Production became about 1.976 times as large from 1971 to 2020.
  2. Undo the 49th power. Raise both sides to the 1/49 power: 1 + r = (1.975741533)1/49 ≈ 1.013993822.
  3. Subtract 1. r ≈ 0.013993822.
  4. Convert to a percent, then round. 0.013993822 × 100 ≈ 1.3993822%, which rounds to 1.399%.

On a calculator, one reliable entry is (4.6 ÷ 2.328239764)^(1 ÷ 49) − 1

Round the percent, not the decimal. Rounding 0.013993822 to 0.014 first gives 1.4%, which is less precise than the three decimal places asked for.

About 1.399% per year. Check: 2.328239764(1.01399)49 ≈ 4.599, which rounds to 4.6.

Watch for these errors

  • Using 7.6 instead of 0.076.
  • Forgetting to convert 500 million to 0.5 billion.
  • Using the wrong number of years: 1950 to 1971 is 21; 1971 to 2020 is 49.
  • Rounding too early in Part D. Use the unrounded Part A value.
