Math Quiz Prep: Linear, Exponential & Logistic Growth

1. Defining Growth Types

Question: Explain the fundamental difference between how a quantity changes in linear growth versus exponential growth.

Answer:

  • Linear Growth: Adds a constant amount per time unit. The rate of change is constant. Example: Saving exactly $50 each month.
  • Exponential Growth: Multiplies by a constant factor (or percentage) per time unit. The rate of change increases as the quantity increases. Example: A population growing by 5% each year.

2. Identifying Growth Type

Question: Identify the growth type for each scenario:

Scenario A: A town's population increases by 1,000 people every year.

Answer: Linear (constant amount added).

Scenario B: A company's profit increases by 12% each year.

Answer: Exponential (constant percentage increase).

3. Calculating Exponential Growth

Question: A mold culture starts with 50 spores and doubles every 6 hours. How many spores will there be after 24 hours?

Solution:

  1. Determine the number of doubling periods: 24 hours / 6 hours/period = 4 periods.
  2. Calculate the final amount: Initial Amount × (Factor)^(Number of Periods)
  3. Calculation: 50 × (2)⁴ = 50 × 16 = 800 spores.

Answer: There will be 800 spores after 24 hours.

4. Using the Rule of 70

Question: If an investment is growing at an average rate of 5% per year, use the Rule of 70 to estimate how long it will take for the investment to double.

Formula:

Doubling Time ≈ 70 / (percentage growth rate)

Calculation:

70 / 5 = 14

Answer: It will take approximately 14 years to double.

5. Understanding Carrying Capacity

Question: Imagine deer living in a forest. What factors might limit the deer population from growing infinitely? What term do we use for the maximum sustainable population size in that environment?

Answer:

  • Limiting Factors: Availability of food, water, space; presence of predators; disease.
  • Term: The maximum sustainable population size is called the carrying capacity (K).
  • Logistic growth models populations that are limited by carrying capacity.

6. Comparing Doubling Time Formulas

Question: When might it be acceptable to use the quick estimate from the Rule of 70, and when might you need a more precise formula (using logarithms)?

Answer:

  • Rule of 70: Good for quick mental estimates, especially for common growth rates (e.g., 1% to 10%). It's an approximation.
  • Precise Formula (e.g., ln(2)/ln(1+r)): Needed for accurate calculations, scientific work, or when dealing with very high or very low growth rates where the Rule of 70 is less accurate.

7. Explaining Logistic Growth

Question: Describe the shape of a logistic growth curve. What happens to the growth rate during the different phases?

Answer:

  • Shape: It's an S-shaped curve.
  • Phases & Growth Rate:
    • Starts slow (initial small population).
    • Accelerates to a maximum growth rate (resembles exponential growth phase).
    • Slows down as it approaches the carrying capacity (resource limitation).
    • Levels off at the carrying capacity (growth rate approaches zero).

8. Calculating Population Growth Rate

Question: A country has 25 births per 1,000 people and 10 deaths per 1,000 people annually. What is its natural population growth rate as a percentage?

Solution:

  1. Find the net increase per 1,000 people: Birth Rate - Death Rate = 25 - 10 = 15 per 1,000.
  2. Convert this rate to a decimal: 15 / 1000 = 0.015.
  3. Convert the decimal to a percentage: 0.015 × 100% = 1.5%.

Answer: The population growth rate is 1.5% per year.

9. Applying Growth Concepts

Question: Give one real-world example where linear growth might be a suitable model, and one where exponential growth is more appropriate (at least initially).

Answer Examples:

  • Linear: Water level rising in a cylindrical tank being filled at a constant rate; Salary increasing by a fixed amount ($2000) each year.
  • Exponential: Compound interest earned on savings; The initial spread of a virus in a large, susceptible population; Bacteria population doubling under ideal conditions.

10. Exponential Calculation Practice

Question: If you start with $500 and it grows by 8% each year, how much money will you have after 3 years?

Solution Method 1 (Step-by-step):

  • Year 1: $500 × 1.08 = $540
  • Year 2: $540 × 1.08 = $583.20
  • Year 3: $583.20 × 1.08 = $629.856

Solution Method 2 (Formula):

Amount = Principal × (1 + rate)^time

Amount = $500 × (1 + 0.08)³ = $500 × (1.08)³ ≈ $500 × 1.259712 ≈ $629.856

Answer: You will have approximately $629.86 after 3 years.
