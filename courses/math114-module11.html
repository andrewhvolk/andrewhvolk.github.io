Exponential & Logistic Growth

Some quantities grow by a constant amount; others by a constant percentage. This module explores exponential growth, how fast it doubles, and what eventually slows it down.

Learn the Concepts

1 · Linear vs. exponential growth

  • Linear growth adds a fixed amount each period (saving $50 a month). A straight line.
  • Exponential growth multiplies by a fixed percentage each period (a population growing 5% a year). It curves upward ever more steeply.

The future value after t periods of exponential growth is:

A = P · (1 + r)t  (or repeated doubling: A = P · 2(number of doublings))

Learn the Concepts

2 · Doubling time and limits to growth

The Rule of 70 gives a quick estimate of how long an exponentially growing quantity takes to double:

doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ (percent growth rate)

Real growth can't continue forever. Logistic growth starts out roughly exponential, then slows and levels off at the environment's carrying capacity, the maximum the resources can sustain. Its graph is an S-shaped curve.

Learn the Concepts

3 · Doubling time and half-life for any amount of time

If a quantity has doubling time T, or half-life T, then after time t:

growth: y = y0 × 2t/T
decay: y = y0 × (1/2)t/T
  • The exponent t/T counts periods. It does not have to be a whole number: 12 years with an 8-year doubling time is 1.5 doublings.
  • Put t and T in the same units before you divide. If the doubling time is in minutes, change hours to minutes first.
  • Count doublings, not days or squares. Quadrupling takes 2 doubling times, and growing 8-fold takes 3. Before you compute, decide which day or square has zero doublings.
  • Doubling is 100% growth. On a chessboard with 1 grain on the first square, square n holds 2n−1 grains, and the first n squares hold 2n − 1 grains in total.

Try it: on the chessboard demo, click square 10 and check that the first 10 squares hold 210 − 1 = 1,023 grains. In the penny-doubling pay demo, day 1 pays one penny (zero doublings), so day 15 pays 214 pennies. If the first penny had already doubled once by day 1, day 15 would pay 215.

Learn the Concepts

4 · Percent decay: absolute vs. relative change

  • Linear change is an absolute change: the quantity rises or falls by the same amount each period.
  • Exponential change is a relative change: the quantity rises or falls by the same percent each period.

A quantity that falls by r percent each period is multiplied by the decay factor 1 − r (with r as a decimal). A 15% yearly loss means multiplying by 0.85 each year.

y = y0 × (1 − r)t
approximate half-life ≈ 70 ÷ (percent decay rate)

Learn the Concepts

5 · Using the Rule of 70, and when to trust it

  • The Rule of 70 is reliable for growth rates below about 15%. Above that it underestimates the doubling time: at 25% a year it gives 2.8 years, but the exact doubling time is about 3.1 years.
  • Once you know the doubling time, the growth factor over any time t is 2t/T.
  • A monthly rate gives a doubling time in months. Convert years to months before computing t/T.
growth factor over time t = 2t/T
exact doubling time T = ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r)

Learn the Concepts

6 · Powers of ten and rates of change

  • If x is between the whole numbers n and n + 1, then 10x is between 10n and 10n+1. For example, 102.7 is between 102 = 100 and 103 = 1000.
  • To turn a yearly change into a smaller unit, divide by the number of those units in a year: 365 days, 8,760 hours, or 525,600 minutes.

Worked Examples

See it done, step by step

Example 1: 50 spores double every 6 hours. How many after 24 hours?

First count the doublings: 24 ÷ 6 = 4.

A = 50 × 24 = 50 × 16 = 800 spores.

Example 2: An investment grows 5% per year. Estimate its doubling time.

Use the Rule of 70.

70 ÷ 5 = about 14 years.

Example 3: $500 grows 8% per year. How much after 3 years?

Use A = P(1 + r)t.

A = 500 × (1.08)3 = 500 × 1.259712 ≈ $629.86.

Example 4: A town of 5,000 people doubles every 8 years. What is its population after 12 years?

Count the doubling periods: 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5. The number of periods does not have to be whole.

Use y = y0 × 2t/T.

y = 5,000 × 21.5 ≈ 5,000 × 2.8284 ≈ 14,142 people.

Example 5: A culture of 100 cells doubles every 20 minutes. How many cells are there after 1.5 hours?

Match the units first: 1.5 hours = 90 minutes.

Count the doubling periods: 90 ÷ 20 = 4.5.

y = 100 × 24.5 ≈ 100 × 22.627 ≈ 2,263 cells.

Example 6: Prices rise 0.5% per month. Use the Rule of 70 to find the doubling time and the growth factor over 5 years. Is the rule valid here?

Doubling time: 70 ÷ 0.5 = 140 months. The rate is monthly, so the doubling time is in months.

Match the units: 5 years = 60 months.

Growth factor = 2t/T = 260/140.

Doubling time ≈ 140 months; growth factor ≈ 1.35 (prices rise about 35%). The rule is valid, since 0.5% is well below 15%.

Example 7: A 500 mg dose of a medicine has a half-life of 6 hours. How much remains after 15 hours?

Count the half-life periods: 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5.

Use y = y0 × (1/2)t/T.

y = 500 × (1/2)2.5 ≈ 500 × 0.17678 ≈ 88.39 mg.

Example 8: Equipment worth $8,000 loses 15% of its value each year. Is the decay linear or exponential, and what is it worth after 3 years?

The value falls by the same percent each year, so this is a relative change: the decay is exponential.

Decay factor: 1 − 0.15 = 0.85.

y = 8,000 × 0.853 = 8,000 × 0.614125 = $4,913.00.

Check Yourself

Quick self-check

1. A population grows 7% per year. Estimate its doubling time.

70 ÷ 7 = about 10 years.

2. Saving exactly $100 every month: linear or exponential?

Linear, because a constant amount is added each period.

3. What does carrying capacity describe?

The maximum population the environment can sustain, where logistic growth levels off.

4. A quantity doubles every 5 years. How long until it quadruples?

Quadrupling is 2 doublings, so 10 years.

5. Is 105.2 between 10,000 and 100,000, or between 100,000 and 1,000,000?

Since 5.2 is between 5 and 6, 105.2 is between 105 and 106: between 100,000 and 1,000,000.

6. A population grows by 3 million people per year. About how many is that per minute?

3,000,000 ÷ 525,600 ≈ 5.7 people per minute.

7. Would you trust the Rule of 70 for 25% annual growth?

No. The rule gives 70 ÷ 25 = 2.8 years, but the exact doubling time, ln 2 ÷ ln 1.25, is about 3.1 years. The rule works best below about 15%.
