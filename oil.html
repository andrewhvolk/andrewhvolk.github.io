Exponential Growth

Oil Production Problem
Step by Step

Goal: use the exponential growth model to estimate production at different growth rates, then compare those estimates to the actual 2020 value.

Slide 1
Problem Information

What are we given?

Starting point

In 1950, production was about 500 million tons.

500 million = 0.5 billion

Growth information

From 1950 to 1971, the growth rate was 7.6% per year.

In 2020, actual production was about 4.6 billion tons.

Slide 2
Main Tool

Use the exponential growth model

A = P(1 + r)t

P = starting amount

r = annual growth rate as a decimal

t = number of years

A = ending amount

Important: 7.6% becomes 0.076, and 3.1% becomes 0.031.
Slide 3
Part A

Estimate production in 1971

  1. Start with 1950 production: P = 0.5 billion tons.
  2. Use the 7.6% growth rate: r = 0.076.
  3. Count the years: 1971 − 1950 = 21, so t = 21.
A = 0.5(1.076)21
Slide 4
Part A Calculation

Calculate and round

A = 0.5(1.076)21

A ≈ 2.328

Rounded to one decimal place: 2.3

Part A answer: approximately 2.3 billion tons in 1971.
Slide 5
Part B

What if 7.6% continued until 2020?

  1. Start from the rounded Part A result: P = 2.3 billion tons.
  2. Use the same growth rate: r = 0.076.
  3. Count the years: 2020 − 1971 = 49, so t = 49.
A = 2.3(1.076)49
Slide 6
Part B Calculation

Calculate and compare

A = 2.3(1.076)49 ≈ 83.3

Part B answer: 83.3 billion tons; the actual figure is far less than this estimate.

Part C

What if the rate after 1971 was 3.1%?

  1. Start from the rounded Part A result: P = 2.3 billion tons.
  2. Use the new growth rate: r = 0.031.
  3. Use the same time span: t = 49 years.
A = 2.3(1.031)49
Slide 8
Part C Calculation

Part C answer: 10.3 billion tons; the actual figure is far less than this estimate.
Slide 9
Part D

What are we solving for?

Part D asks for the annual growth rate from 1971 to 2020 that produces the actual 2020 value.

Known values

1971: about 2.328 billion tons

2020: 4.6 billion tons

Unknown value

r = annual growth rate

t = 2020 − 1971 = 49 years

Slide 10
Part D Setup

Use the unrounded 1971 value

The 1971 estimate came from Part A:

0.5(1.076)21 ≈ 2.328239764

The answer box in Part A rounds this to 2.3, but Part D needs more precision to get the expected rate.

Using 2.3 instead of 2.328239764 gives a slightly different rate.
Slide 11
Part D Equation

Build the exponential equation

Start at the 1971 amount and grow for 49 years until reaching the actual 2020 amount.

4.6 = 2.328239764(1 + r)49
  1. 4.6 is the ending amount.
  2. 2.328239764 is the starting amount.
  3. 49 is the number of years.
Slide 12
Part D Algebra

Isolate the growth factor

Divide both sides by the starting amount.

4.6 ÷ 2.328239764 ≈ 1.975741533

(1 + r)49 ≈ 1.975741533

This means production became about 1.976 times as large from 1971 to 2020.

Slide 13
Part D Algebra

Undo the 49th power

To undo a 49th power, raise both sides to the 1/49 power.

1 + r = (1.975741533)1/49

1 + r ≈ 1.013993822

r ≈ 1.013993822 − 1 = 0.013993822

Slide 14
Part D Calculator Help

Calculator sequence

One reliable way to enter the calculation is:

(4.6 ÷ 2.328239764)^(1 ÷ 49) − 1

≈ 0.013993822

Then convert the decimal rate to a percent:

0.013993822 × 100 ≈ 1.3993822%

Slide 17
Part D Rounding

Round the percent, not the decimal rate

Exact rate: r ≈ 0.0139938215

As a percent: 0.0139938215 × 100 ≈ 1.39938215%

Rounded to three decimal places: 1.399%
Do not round 0.0139938215 to 0.014 first. That would become 1.4%, which is less precise than the requested answer.
Slide 18
Part D Check

Check the final rate

Substitute the rounded rate back into the model.

2.328239764(1.01399)49 ≈ 4.599

4.599 billion rounds to 4.6 billion.

Part D answer: approximately 1.399% per year.
Slide 19
Common Mistakes

Watch for these errors

  • Using 7.6 instead of 0.076.
  • Forgetting to convert 500 million to 0.5 billion.
  • Using the wrong number of years: 1950 to 1971 is 21; 1971 to 2020 is 49.
  • Rounding too early in Part D. Use the unrounded Part A value when possible.
