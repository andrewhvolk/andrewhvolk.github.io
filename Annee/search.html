Start Here: This Is Not a Broad MASLD Review

First target: 10 candidate sources

Your first job is not to master the whole MASLD field. Your first job is to find a small, high-quality seed set of sources that justify this study’s method: comparing diagnosis codes against computable biomarker indices in a large EHR/biobank cohort.

One-sentence project summary: This study asks whether MASLD is severely underdiagnosed in the All of Us Research Program by comparing ICD-coded diagnoses with likely disease identified through FLI/HSI and fibrosis risk identified through FIB-4.

You are not expected to…

Diagnose liver disease or make clinical recommendations.
Read thousands of search results.
Understand every formula mathematically before starting.
Decide final inclusion alone before the first mentor checkpoint.
Use every database on day one.
Write the literature review before building the article set.

Important stop rule: Do not continue searching once you have 10 plausible candidate sources across the required buckets. Bring those to the mentor checkpoint first.

Study Scope

Purpose

To quantify underdiagnosis of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) within the All of Us Research Program by contrasting electronic health record diagnosis codes against non-invasive biomarker-index phenotypes.

Research Questions

Research QuestionWhat the literature review must support
RQ1. What is the absolute difference in MASLD prevalence when identified via ICD-10 codes versus continuous biomarker indices like FLI? ICD codes undercapture NAFLD/MASLD; FLI/HSI can serve as computable steatosis phenotypes in population-scale data.
RQ2. What proportion of the un-coded, seemingly healthy population has FIB-4 scores indicating indeterminate or high risk for advanced fibrosis? FIB-4 is a widely used first-pass non-invasive fibrosis-risk tool that can be calculated from routine labs and age.
RQ3. How do cumulative SDOH disadvantages influence a patient’s likelihood of remaining in the un-coded diagnostic gap? Diagnosis, screening, referral, and coding may be affected by access, deprivation, insurance, socioeconomic status, or other SDOH-related barriers.

Methodological Center

The literature review should support the planned cross-sectional analysis of All of Us OMOP data: condition records for ICD-coded MASLD/NAFLD, measurement and physical-measurement data for index calculation, and survey/observation data for SDOH variables.

Screening Rules: Keep, Maybe, or Toss

Keep

  • Compares ICD-coded NAFLD/MASLD against expected or biomarker-based prevalence.
  • Uses EHR, claims, primary care, registry, cohort, or biobank data.
  • Uses FLI, HSI, FIB-4, or related non-invasive indices in large-scale data.
  • Links SDOH or access to diagnosis, screening, referral, or recognition.
  • Explains All of Us, OMOP, EHR tables, or computable phenotyping.

Maybe

  • General MASLD/NAFLD prevalence review.
  • Terminology/nomenclature source.
  • Imaging validation paper that helps interpret FLI/HSI.
  • Disparities paper focused on outcomes rather than diagnosis.
  • Machine-learning paper only if it compares against ICD, FLI/HSI, or FIB-4.

Toss

  • Drug trials for NASH/MASH treatment.
  • Animal or basic-science liver studies.
  • Pediatric-only studies.
  • Bariatric or surgical cohorts only, unless used for validation.
  • Genetics-only papers, unless directly connected to missed diagnosis.
  • General disease-background papers after you already have 1–2.

Writing Map for the Literature Review

Once extraction is complete, organize the literature review around the study logic, not around every topic in MASLD.

  1. Clinical and public-health importance of MASLD. Keep this brief. Use only 2–3 sources.
  2. Problem: ICD-coded diagnosis undercaptures true disease burden. This is the main diagnostic-gap section.
  3. Methodological solution: computable steatosis phenotypes. Explain why FLI/HSI are useful in large datasets when imaging is unavailable.
  4. Hidden fibrosis risk among uncoded patients. Explain why FIB-4 matters for identifying indeterminate/high-risk participants.
  5. SDOH and the risk of remaining invisible in the medical record. Explain how access, socioeconomic factors, and care pathways may influence coding/diagnosis.
  6. All of Us and OMOP feasibility. Explain why All of Us is suitable for this cross-sectional EHR/biomarker-index study.
  7. Gap addressed by the current study. Conclude that few studies directly combine ICD-coded MASLD, computable biomarker-index phenotypes, fibrosis-risk scores, and cumulative SDOH disadvantage within All of Us.

Writing reminder: The review does not need to prove that MASLD matters in general. It needs to prove that this study’s comparison is justified and useful.
