Final Exam Review

Comprehensive review covering Quiz 13 vectors, key formulas, and all tested topics.

Overview Formulas Practice Schedule
Comprehensive final. The exam covers all course topics — algebra, geometry, trigonometry, logarithms, applications, and vectors — in roughly 25–30 questions.
Practical takeaway. Review broadly. The exam mixes short skill checks with multi-step application problems, so accuracy in setup matters as much as calculation.

Trig, angles, and circles

Exact trig values from a figure, solving right triangles, arc length, sector area, central angle, degree–radian conversion, DMS conversion, and linear speed are all fair game.

Algebra and equations

Exponent simplification, function evaluation, quadratic equations, rational equations, set notation, and interval union/intersection are covered.

Coordinate geometry

Distance, midpoint, the equation of a line through two points, and the equation of a perpendicular line through the origin can all appear.

Geometry with diagrams

Polygon area, angle-chasing with parallel lines, and angle relationships from diagrams are included — visual reading matters.

Logs, exponentials, and models

Writing logarithmic and exponential forms, solving those equations, compound interest, and applied motion/descent situations are all tested.

Vectors

Vector-resultant problems are part of the final, so Quiz 13 content carries forward into the exam.

Formula focus

Provided on the Final Exam

These formulas are printed on the exam. They still require recognition and correct setup.

Heron’s Formula

$$\mathcal{A}=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$$
Use with $s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}$.

Semiperimeter

$$s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}$$
Pairs naturally with Heron’s Formula.

Arc length

$$s=r\theta$$
Angle $\theta$ must be in radians.

Sector area

$$\mathcal{A}=\frac12 r^2\theta$$
Again, $\theta$ is in radians.

Triangle area

$$\mathcal{A}=\frac12 bc\sin A$$
Useful when two sides and the included angle are known.

Circumference

$$C=2\pi r$$
Helpful with circle relationships and interpretation.

Know these cold

Distance

$$d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$$
Needed for coordinate geometry and applications.

Midpoint

$$M=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$
Shows up directly and supports geometry reasoning.

Compound interest

$$A=P\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \qquad A=Pe^{rt}$$
Be sure you can identify each variable from context.

Change of base

$$\log_b x=\frac{\ln x}{\ln b}=\frac{\log x}{\log b}$$
Part of the official review topics.

Vector components

$$\langle r\cos\theta,\;r\sin\theta\rangle$$
Essential for Quiz 13 and still relevant on the final.

Trig ratios

$$\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}},\quad \cos\theta=\frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}},\quad \tan\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$$
Useful for exact values, solving triangles, and angle applications.

Streamlined for Quiz 13 vectors and the comprehensive final exam.

Quiz 13 is Section 8.4: Vectors. The quiz covers component form, magnitude, direction, graphical vector addition, and resultant vectors.
components magnitude direction vector addition
Fast strategy. Translate everything into components as early as possible. Once the vector is in component form, magnitude and direction become routine.

Problem 1

Given a vector drawn on the coordinate plane, find its component form $\langle v_1, v_2 \rangle$, then compute direction $\theta$ and magnitude $\|v\|$.

Problem 2

Add vectors graphically by drawing the resultant, then state the resultant components $\langle r_1, r_2 \rangle$ for each graph.

Problem 3

Convert vectors from magnitude-and-direction form to components, add them, then find $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$, $\|\mathbf{r}\|$, and $\theta_r$.

Core vector moves

Quiz 13 essentials

Component form

$$\mathbf{v}=\langle a,b\rangle$$
Read the horizontal and vertical change carefully from tail to head.

Magnitude

$$\|\mathbf{v}\|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$$
Use exact work first, then round only when requested.

Direction from components

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)$$
Adjust for quadrant. A calculator angle alone is not always the final direction.

From magnitude and direction

$$\langle r\cos\theta,\;r\sin\theta\rangle$$
This is the standard bridge from polar-style information to components.

Add vectors

$$\langle a,b\rangle+\langle c,d\rangle=\langle a+c,\;b+d\rangle$$
Graphically and algebraically, the same vector rules should agree.

Resultant workflow

$$\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$$
Convert, add, simplify, then find magnitude and direction last.
