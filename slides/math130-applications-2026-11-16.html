Reference-angle rules

Formula focus
  • The reference angle is the positive acute angle to the x-axis.
  • First add or subtract $360^\circ$ (or $2\pi$) to land in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$; then find the quadrant.
QuadrantDegreesRadians
I$\alpha=\theta$$\alpha=\theta$
II$\alpha=180^\circ-\theta$$\alpha=\pi-\theta$
III$\alpha=\theta-180^\circ$$\alpha=\theta-\pi$
IV$\alpha=360^\circ-\theta$$\alpha=2\pi-\theta$

Sketch, then find the reference angle

Sketch each angle in standard position and show its reference angle: $-135^\circ$, $1035^\circ$, $5\pi/3$.

  1. $-135^\circ$: turn clockwise into Quadrant III; $\alpha=180^\circ-135^\circ=45^\circ$.
  2. $1035^\circ-720^\circ=315^\circ$ (two full turns): Quadrant IV; $\alpha=360^\circ-315^\circ=45^\circ$.
  3. $3\pi/2<5\pi/3<2\pi$: Quadrant IV; $\alpha=2\pi-5\pi/3=\pi/3$.

Model · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Draw the rotation in one color and the reference arc in another. For 1035 degrees, draw two full loops before the last 315 degrees.

Reference angles in degrees and radians

Student practice

  2. $375^\circ$
  3. $-390^\circ$
  4. $8\pi/9$
  5. $17\pi/12$
Feedback and solution
  • $200^\circ$: Quadrant III, $\alpha=20^\circ$.
  • $375^\circ-360^\circ=15^\circ$: Quadrant I, $\alpha=15^\circ$.
  • $-390^\circ+720^\circ=330^\circ$: Quadrant IV, $\alpha=30^\circ$.
  • $\pi/2<8\pi/9<\pi$: Quadrant II, $\alpha=\pi-8\pi/9=\pi/9$.
  • $\pi<17\pi/12<3\pi/2$: Quadrant III, $\alpha=17\pi/12-\pi=5\pi/12$.

Which functions are positive?

Concept
  • Quadrant I: all six are positive. Quadrant II: sine and cosecant.
  • Quadrant III: tangent and cotangent. Quadrant IV: cosine and secant.
  • Cosecant has the sign of sine, secant of cosine, cotangent of tangent.
  • Two sign clues: list the quadrants for each clue, then keep the one in both lists.

Explain · 2 minSource: Authored

  • Tie each sign to the coordinate: cosine follows x, sine follows y.

Two sign clues, one quadrant

Student practice
  1. $\cos\theta<0$ and $\csc\theta<0$
  2. $\sin\theta<0$ and $\cot\theta>0$
  3. $\cos\theta>0$ and $\tan\theta<0$
  4. $\cos\theta>0$ and $\sin\theta>0$
  5. $\tan\theta>0$ and $\sin\theta>0$
  6. $\cos\theta>0$ and $\sin\theta<0$
Feedback and solution
  • II or III, and III or IV: Quadrant III.
  • III or IV, and I or III: Quadrant III.
  • I or IV, and II or IV: Quadrant IV.
  • I or IV, and I or II: Quadrant I.
  • I or III, and I or II: Quadrant I.
  • I or IV, and III or IV: Quadrant IV.

Values from a point on the terminal side

Student practice
  1. Find $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ first; $r$ is always positive.
  2. $(6,5)$: find $\cos\theta$, $\sec\theta$, and $\cot\theta$.
  3. $(-5,-2)$: find $\sin\theta$, $\sec\theta$, and $\tan\theta$.
Feedback and solution
  • $(6,5)$: $r=\sqrt{61}$; $\cos\theta=6/\sqrt{61}=6\sqrt{61}/61$, $\sec\theta=\sqrt{61}/6$, $\cot\theta=6/5$.
  • $(-5,-2)$: $r=\sqrt{29}$; $\sin\theta=-2/\sqrt{29}=-2\sqrt{29}/29$, $\sec\theta=-\sqrt{29}/5$, $\tan\theta=2/5$.
  • Check: $(-5,-2)$ is in Quadrant III, where only tangent and cotangent are positive.

Which ratio?

Predict first

A plane climbs at $9^\circ$. You know its altitude gain and want the distance it flies. Which trig ratio links them?

Commit to a response before discussion.

Feedback and solution

Sine: the altitude is opposite the climb angle, and the distance flown is the hypotenuse.

Activate · 1 minSource: Authored

Climb, descent, and bearings

Concept
  • Speed is along the flight path, so distance flown $=$ speed $\times$ time is the hypotenuse.
  • Climb or descent: altitude $=$ distance flown $\times\sin(\text{angle})$. Convert hours to minutes with $\times60$.
  • A bearing such as S $38^\circ$ W starts at south and turns $38^\circ$ toward west (compass heading $218^\circ$).
  • Because the angle is measured from north or south, the north-south leg uses cosine and the east-west leg uses sine.

Explain · 2 minSource: Authored

Time to climb

A plane climbs at $9^\circ$ at 360 mph along its flight path. How long does it take to gain 5 miles of altitude? Round to the nearest minute.

  1. $\sin9^\circ=5/d$, so $d=5/\sin9^\circ\approx31.96$ mi.
  2. $t=31.96/360\approx0.0888$ hr.
  3. $0.0888\times60\approx5.3$, about 5 minutes.

Model · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Ask why tangent would give the ground distance instead of the distance flown.

Altitude lost on a descent

Student practice

A plane descends at 260 mph at an angle of depression of $3^\circ$. How much altitude does it lose in 8 minutes? Round to the nearest tenth of a mile.

Feedback and solution
  • Distance flown $=260\times8/60\approx34.67$ mi.
  • Altitude lost $=34.67\sin3^\circ\approx1.8$ mi.

Practice · 3 minSource: Authored

How far south and west?

A plane flies 280 knots on a bearing of S $38^\circ$ W for 1.8 hours. How many nautical miles south and west has it traveled?

  1. Total distance $=280\times1.8=504$ NM.
  2. South $=504\cos38^\circ\approx397$ NM.
  3. West $=504\sin38^\circ\approx310$ NM.

Model · 3 minSource: Authored

  • Sketch the compass first. The 38 degree angle sits at the south axis, so south is adjacent.

The way home

Student practice
  1. A plane flies north for 1.5 hours, then west for 0.75 hour, at 350 mph on both legs.
  2. Find the direct distance back to the airport and the bearing the plane should fly.
Feedback and solution
  • Legs: north $350\times1.5=525$ mi, west $350\times0.75=262.5$ mi.
  • Distance $=\sqrt{525^2+262.5^2}\approx587.0$ mi.
  • $\tan A=262.5/525$, so $A\approx26.6^\circ$. The airport is south and east of the plane: bearing S $26.6^\circ$ E.

Practice · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Require a sketch with both legs labeled before any calculation.

Bearing and time mistakes

Feedback and solution

  • 0.0888 is in hours; multiply by 60 to report about 5 minutes.
