convert angle measures and compute arc length and sector area

Formula focus
  • Radians measure arc length per radius.
  • $s=r\theta$ and $A=\tfrac12r^2\theta$ require radians.
  • For inverse problems, solve $r=s/\theta$ or $\theta=2A/r^2$ before substituting.
  • Convert degrees using $\theta_{rad}=\theta_{deg}\pi/180$.

Explain · 2.5 minSource: 14, 15

Model the Skill: convert angle measures and compute arc length and sector area

1

$120^\circ=2\pi/3$ radians.

2

$s=r\theta=12(2\pi/3)=8\pi\approx25.133$ units.

3

$A=\tfrac12r^2\theta=48\pi\approx150.796$ square units.

Model · 3.0 minSource: 16, 17

You Try: convert angle measures and compute arc length and sector area

Student practice
  1. Find the arc length for radius 9 and central angle 40°.
  2. An arc has length $6\pi$ units and central angle $2\pi/5$ radians. Solve for the radius.
  3. A sector has radius 5 units and area $25\pi/6$ square units. Find its central angle in radians and degrees.
  4. Convert 135° to radians.
  5. Convert $67.425^\circ$ to DMS and $22^\circ18'36''$ to decimal degrees.
Feedback and solution
  • $40^\circ=2\pi/9$ radians, so $s=9(2\pi/9)=2\pi$.
  • $r=s/\theta=6\pi/(2\pi/5)=15$ units.
  • $25\pi/6=\tfrac12(5^2)\theta$ gives $\theta=\pi/3=60^\circ$.
  • $135^\circ=3\pi/4$ radians.
  • $67.425^\circ=67^\circ25'30''$; $22^\circ18'36''=22.31^\circ$.

Practice · 6.0 minSource: 18

  • Ask students to name the unknown before choosing an equation.
  • For DMS, use 60 seconds per minute and 60 minutes per degree; pause before revealing solutions.

Check and Diagnose: convert angle measures and compute arc length and sector area

Error analysis
  • Do not insert degrees directly into radian formulas.
  • For an arc, isolate $r=s/\theta$ when radius is unknown; for a sector, isolate $\theta=2A/r^2$.
  • Sector area should be the same fraction of $\pi r^2$ as the angle is of a full rotation.

relate angular velocity, linear velocity, and distance

Formula focus
  • Angular speed: $\omega=\theta/t$.
  • Linear speed: $v=r\omega$.
  • Distance: $d=vt=r\theta$.
  • For rpm, multiply by 360°/60 to get degrees per second; use 2π/60 to get radians per second for $v=rω$.

Explain · 2.5 minSource: 23

Model the Skill: relate angular velocity, linear velocity, and distance

1

$v=r\omega=4(3)=12$ ft/s.

2

$d=vt=12(10)=120$ ft.

3

Radians are dimensionless in the linear-speed conversion.

Model · 3.0 minSource: 24

You Try: relate angular velocity, linear velocity, and distance

Student practice
  1. A wheel of radius 0.35 m turns at 8 rad/s. Find rim speed.
  2. A wheel of radius 3 ft turns at 18 revolutions per minute. Find angular speed in degrees per second and rim speed in feet per second.
Feedback and solution
  • $v=r\omega=0.35(8)=2.8$ m/s.
  • $18(360)/60=108^\circ/s$; $108^\circ/s=3\pi/5$ rad/s, so $v=3(3\pi/5)=9\pi/5\approx5.7$ ft/s.

Practice · 5.0 minSource: Authored

  • Require units in both the converted angular speed and linear speed.
  • Pause before revealing the two solutions.

Check and Diagnose: relate angular velocity, linear velocity, and distance

Error analysis
  • Angular units must be radians for $v=r\omega$.
  • For rev/min, convert to degrees per second with 360/60 and radians per second with 2π/60 before using $v=rω$.
  • Check that linear speed units include distance per time.

select volume and surface-area formulas for solids

1

Volume measures three-dimensional capacity; surface area measures exposed two-dimensional faces.

2

Cylinder: $V=\pi r^2h$ and $SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.

Explain · 2.5 minSource: 27

3

Subtract the inner cylinder from the outer cylinder.
