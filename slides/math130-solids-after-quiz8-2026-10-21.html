Cylinder volume and surface-area formulas

Formula focus
  • Base area and circumference: $A_{\mathrm{base}}=\pi r^2$, $C=2\pi r$.
  • Volume (capacity): $V=A_{\mathrm{base}}h=\pi r^2h$. Units are cubic.
  • Lateral area (curved side): $LA=Ch=2\pi rh$.
  • Closed total surface: $SA=2A_{\mathrm{base}}+LA$ (two bases plus curved side).
  • Expanded form: $SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$. Units are square.

Explain · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Point to each surface part as its term appears. For an open cylinder, include only the surfaces that are present.

Model: diameter given, radius required

A closed cylinder has diameter $d=10$ cm and height $h=8$ cm. Find exact and approximate volume and total surface area.

  1. The given measure is the diameter, so $r=d/2=10/2=5$ cm.
  2. Volume: $V=\pi r^2h=\pi(5)^2(8)=200\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx628.32\text{ cm}^3$.
  3. Two bases: $2\pi r^2=50\pi\text{ cm}^2$. Curved side: $LA=2\pi rh=80\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
  4. Total: $SA=50\pi+80\pi=130\pi\text{ cm}^2\approx408.41\text{ cm}^2$.

Model · 5 minSource: Authored

  • Pause after the radius conversion; have students predict which result needs square units and which needs cubic units.

You try: closed-cylinder volume and area

Student practice

A closed cylinder has radius $r=3$ cm and height $h=10$ cm. Find exact and approximate volume and total surface area. Show the two surface-area contributions.

Feedback and solution
  • $V=\pi(3)^2(10)=90\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx282.74\text{ cm}^3$.
  • Two bases: $2\pi(3)^2=18\pi\text{ cm}^2$; curved side: $2\pi(3)(10)=60\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
  • $SA=18\pi+60\pi=78\pi\text{ cm}^2\approx245.04\text{ cm}^2$.

Practice · 5 minSource: Authored

  • Give students quiet think time before comparing setups.

Check the setup before the arithmetic

Use the radius in both formulas; when the problem gives diameter, divide it by two first.

Volume uses cubic units. Surface area uses square units and counts only the surfaces that are present.

Extension: material in a hollow cylinder

A pipe segment has outer radius $R=4$ cm, inner radius $r=3$ cm, and length $h=10$ cm. Find the volume of material.

  1. The material cross-section is an annulus: $A_{\mathrm{material}}=\pi R^2-\pi r^2=\pi(R^2-r^2)$.
  2. Extend that cross-section through length $h$: $V_{\mathrm{material}}=A_{\mathrm{material}}h=\pi(R^2-r^2)h$.
  3. Substitute: $V=\pi(4^2-3^2)(10)=70\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx219.91\text{ cm}^3$.
  4. Subtract the inner circle's area, not the radii; volume uses cubic units.

Model · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Name the outer and inner radii before substituting; the inner radius describes the hollow opening.

Exit check: what is being measured?

A closed cylinder has $r=3$ cm and $h=7$ cm. One student labels its capacity $63\pi$ cm$^2$ and its total surface area $60\pi$ cm$^3$. Identify and correct the mix-up.

Exit response

Answer in complete mathematical sentences and identify one remaining question.

Feedback and solution
  • Capacity is volume: $63\pi$ cm$^3$. Total surface area is $60\pi$ cm$^2$. The student switched both the quantities and their units.
