Build the cylinder formulas from its parts

Concept
  • The circular base has area $\pi r^2$. Stacking that area through height $h$ gives volume: $V=\pi r^2h$, measured in cubic units.
  • A closed cylinder unwraps to two circles and one rectangle: $SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$, measured in square units.

Explain · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Label radius and height before substitution. Total surface area includes both circular ends.

Model: diameter given, radius required

A closed cylinder has diameter 10 cm and height 8 cm. Find its exact volume and total surface area.

  1. Convert diameter to radius: $r=10/2=5$ cm.
  2. Volume: $V=\pi(5)^2(8)=200\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx628.32\text{ cm}^3$.
  3. Total surface area: $SA=2\pi(5)^2+2\pi(5)(8)=130\pi\text{ cm}^2\approx408.41\text{ cm}^2$.

Model · 5 minSource: Authored

  • Pause after the radius conversion; have students predict whether the answers need square or cubic units.
Pacing checkpoint

You try: closed-cylinder volume and area

Student practice

A closed cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Find its exact volume and total surface area. Include units.

Feedback and solution
  • $V=\pi(3)^2(10)=90\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx282.74\text{ cm}^3$.
  • $SA=2\pi(3)^2+2\pi(3)(10)=78\pi\text{ cm}^2\approx245.04\text{ cm}^2$.

Practice · 5 minSource: Authored

  • Give students quiet think time before comparing setups.

Check the setup before the arithmetic

Use the radius in both formulas; when the problem gives diameter, divide it by two first.

Volume uses cubic units. Surface area uses square units and counts only the surfaces that are present.

Extension: material in a hollow cylinder

A pipe segment has outer radius 4 cm, inner radius 3 cm, and length 10 cm. Find the volume of material.

  1. Material cross-section is the outer circle minus the inner circle: $V=\pi(R^2-r^2)h$.
  2. $V=\pi(4^2-3^2)(10)=70\pi\text{ cm}^3\approx219.91\text{ cm}^3$.
  3. Subtract the hollow interior; keep the final unit cubic because the result is volume.

Model · 4 minSource: Authored

  • Connect the subtraction to a picture of an outer cylinder with the hollow center removed.

Exit check: what is being measured?

A closed cylinder has $r=3$ cm and $h=7$ cm. One student labels its capacity $63\pi$ cm$^2$ and its total surface area $60\pi$ cm$^3$. Identify and correct the mix-up.

Exit response

Answer in complete mathematical sentences and identify one remaining question.

Feedback and solution
  • Capacity is volume: $63\pi$ cm$^3$. Total surface area is $60\pi$ cm$^2$. The student switched both the quantities and their units.
  • Keep the final five minutes flexible for completion, correction, or a second radius-versus-diameter check.
