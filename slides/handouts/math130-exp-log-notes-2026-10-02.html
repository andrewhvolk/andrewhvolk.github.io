Part 1 · Exponential functions (Section 4.2)

An exponential function has the form $f(x)=b^x$ where $b>0$ and $b\ne$ $1$.

y-intercept: $(0,1)$

Horizontal asymptote: $y=0$

Domain: $(-\infty,\infty)$

Range: $(0,\infty)$

If $b>1$ the graph shows growth; if $0<b<1$ the graph shows decay.

Three interest models

$P$ = principal, $r$ = annual rate as a decimal, $t$ = years, $n$ = compounding periods per year.

ModelFormulaOn Test 2?
Simple interest$A=P(1+rt)$Not printed. Memorize.
Compound interest$A=P\left(1+\tfrac rn\right)^{nt}$Printed on the test.
Continuous compounding$A=Pe^{rt}$Printed on the test.

Common values of $n$: annually $1$, quarterly $4$, monthly $12$, daily $365$.

Whiteboard Example 2

$\$2000$ is invested at $5\%$ for $6$ years. Find the balance with (a) simple interest, (b) interest compounded quarterly, and (c) interest compounded continuously.

(a) $A=2000(1+0.05\cdot6)=2000(1.3)=\$2600.00$

(b) $A=2000\left(1+\tfrac{0.05}{4}\right)^{4\cdot6}=2000(1.0125)^{24}\approx\$2694.70$

(c) $A=2000e^{0.05\cdot6}=2000e^{0.3}\approx\$2699.72$

Order: simple $<$ quarterly $<$ continuous. Round only at the final step.

Growth by $r$ per period: multiply by $1+r$    Decay by $r$ per period: multiply by $1-r$

Part 2 · Logarithms (Section 4.3)

Key idea: a logarithm is an exponent. $\log_b x$ answers the question “$b$ to what power gives $x$?”

$\log_b x = y \quad\iff\quad$ $b^y = x$

$\log x$ means base $10$ (common log)

$\ln x$ means base $e$ (natural log)

Restrictions: the base satisfies $b>0$, $b\ne1$, and the input satisfies $x$ $>0$.
