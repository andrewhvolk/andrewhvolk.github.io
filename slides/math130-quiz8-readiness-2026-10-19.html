Angles and intercepted arcs

Concept
  • A central angle equals the measure of its intercepted arc.
  • An inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc.
  • All arcs around a circle total 360°.

Explain · 1 minSource: Authored

Central and inscribed angles

Find angle ABD and minor arc AD.

  1. The central angle AOD is 124°, so minor arc AD is 124°.
  2. Angle ABD intercepts that same arc: $124^\circ/2=62^\circ$.
  3. The vertex location determines the rule.

Angle conversion rules

Formula focus
  • $1^\circ=60\prime$ and $1\prime=60\prime\prime$.
  • DMS to decimal: $D+M/60+S/3600$.
  • Degrees to radians: multiply by $\pi/180$.
  • Radians to degrees: multiply by $180/\pi$.

Explain · 1 minSource: Authored

Decimal degrees to DMS

Convert 38.726145° to DMS, to the nearest second.

  1. Degrees: 38°. Multiply the decimal part by 60: $0.726145(60)=43.5687$.
  2. Minutes: 43′. Multiply the remaining decimal by 60: $0.5687(60)=34.122$.
  3. Round once, at the end: 38°43′34″.

Arc and sector formulas

Formula focus
  • Arc length: $s=r\theta$. Sector area: $A=\tfrac12r^2\theta$.
  • Both formulas require θ in radians.
  • Unknown radius: $r=s/\theta$.
  • Unknown sector angle: $\theta=2A/r^2$, then convert to degrees if requested.

Explain · 2 minSource: Authored

One sector, two measurements

Radius = 6 units and angle = 1.4 rad. Find arc length and sector area.

  1. $s=6(1.4)=8.4$ units.
  2. $A=\tfrac12(6^2)(1.4)=25.2$ square units.
  3. The radius is squared only in the area formula.

Solve backwards for angle

A sector has area 35.4 square units and radius 4 units. Find θ in radians and degrees.

  1. $\theta=2A/r^2=2(35.4)/4^2=4.425$ rad.
  2. $\theta_{deg}=4.425(180/\pi)\approx253.53^\circ$.
  3. The angle is between π and 2π radians, so a sector larger than a semicircle is reasonable.

From rpm to speed

Formula focus
  • Use radius = diameter ÷ 2.
  • Degrees per second: $n\cdot360/60$.
  • Radians per second: $\omega=n\cdot2\pi/60$.
  • Linear speed: $v=r\omega$ in distance units per second.

Explain · 1.5 minSource: Authored

A rotating wheel

Diameter = 12 ft, rotation = 24 rpm. Find angular speed in degrees/s and rim speed in ft/s.

  1. $r=12/2=6$ ft.
  2. $24(360)/60=144^\circ/\mathrm{s}$.
  3. $\omega=24(2\pi)/60=4\pi/5$ rad/s.
  4. $v=6(4\pi/5)=24\pi/5\approx15.08$ ft/s.

Two common speed errors

Error analysis
  • Using the diameter in $v=r\omega$ doubles the speed.
  • Degrees/s cannot go directly into $v=r\omega$; use radians/s.
  • Convert minutes to seconds once, and label the final units.
