Proposal for a 4+1 Pathway

B.S. in Applied Mathematics & Statistics + M.S. in Applied Statistics

Proposal Draft | May 2026

Program Design & Regulatory Parameters

Parameter Design Specification
Credit overlap Limit shared credit to 12 graduate hours. All other graduate credits remain M.S.-only.
Total unique credits 144 unique credits (120 B.S. + 36 M.S. - 12 shared).
Graduate start point Delay graduate coursework until senior standing (Fall 2029 for the Fall 2026 cohort).
Degree conferral Sequential conferral: B.S. is awarded in Spring 2030, then the student is formally classified as graduate for the remaining M.S. coursework.
Title IV and aid posture Separate statuses. Undergraduate aid applies through B.S. completion; graduate options apply after reclassification.

Title IV & SACSCOC Compliance Findings

By leveraging established academic precedents, this 4+1 model allows students to begin their graduate coursework during their senior year, sharing up to 12 credits across both degrees, rather than waiting until the conferral of their Bachelor's degree to begin M.S. studies. This approach perfectly aligns with SACSCOC and Title IV regulations:

SACSCOC Overlap Precedent

Accreditation guidelines support a clear, limited overlap between undergraduate and graduate work. By establishing a shared, double-counted coursework matrix of exactly 12 credit hours (four courses) during the senior year, the pathway accelerates degree completion. It simultaneously preserves the academic integrity and standalone value of the master's degree, ensuring 24 distinct graduate credits remain post-conferral.

Title IV Financial Aid Clarity

Allowing students to take up to 12 graduate credits while still in their senior year cleanly resolves federal student aid ambiguities. Students retain undergraduate status (preserving Pell Grant and subsidized loan eligibility) through Year 4. Upon B.S. conferral, they are formally reclassified to graduate status for Year 5, appropriately triggering graduate-level loan and assistantship eligibility without regulatory conflict.

External Precedents: SACSCOC-Accredited 4+1 Programs

The proposed model—allowing senior undergraduates to complete up to 12 credits of graduate coursework that double-counts toward both degrees—is a well-established standard among SACSCOC-accredited peer and aspirational institutions. This demonstrates clear regulatory precedent for the pathway's structure.

Virginia Tech

Accelerated UG/GR Degree

Permits accepted students with a 3.3 GPA or higher to take graduate courses during their senior year. The university explicitly allows up to 12 credit hours to be double-counted toward both the bachelor's and master's degrees.

North Carolina State

Accelerated Bachelor's/Master's (ABM)

The ABM program structure allows students to complete up to 12 credit hours of graduate-level coursework that applies to both the undergraduate and graduate degree requirements, pending departmental approval.

George Mason University

Bachelor's/Accelerated Master's (BAM)

Offers BAM pathways across multiple STEM fields, including Statistics and Data Science. Students in advanced standing may share a maximum of 12 graduate credits between their undergraduate and graduate degree audits.

Why STAT 501 is included: STAT 502 and STAT 510 require STAT 501. The most sequence-safe 12-credit shared set must include STAT 501 to prevent prerequisite blocks.

Comparison: Math/Stats Options vs. Existing History Combined Programs

The College of Arts and Sciences already operates combined History pathways (B.A./M.A. and B.S./M.A. in History). They provide a structural precedent for a 4+1 combined pathway with defined admissions gates, a five-year course sequence, and senior-year graduate access. Based on this, two Math/Stats options were analyzed:

Implementation Option Unique Credits Strengths Weaknesses
Option A: Direct no-overlap 4+1 analogue 156-159 (120 B.S. + 36 M.S.) Lowest policy risk; easiest to explain as non-overlapping. Least attractive to students; higher credits due to 36-cr M.S.
Option B: Recommended 12-credit shared 4+1 pathway 144-147 (120 + 36 - 12 shared) More efficient; academically coherent as grad courses replace specific UG stats requirements. Requires explicit approval for 12 shared credits and careful Title IV treatment.
